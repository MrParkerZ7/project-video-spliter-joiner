## Concept

- **What T-219 covers.** T-219 proves SPEC-001 I66 with real-ffmpeg first-frame md5 and frame-count checks on: a B-frame H.264 MP4, a B-frame HEVC MP4, a 10 s GOP, a 3 s clip, an MPEG-TS, and the TS remuxed to MP4 (`tests/Core.Tests/LosslessBoundaryIntegrationTests.cs`).
- **The gap.** Its edge cases say that `.mkv`, `.mov` and `.ts` outputs use the same args, but there is no Matroska or QuickTime source in the matrix.
- **Why it matters.** The bench's `mkv` fixture records a video `start_time` of 0.021 s with audio at 0, the same shape as `4k10m`. The muxer conversion therefore relies on `StreamInfo.StartTime` for Matroska too, which nothing asserts.

## Design

Add shapes to `LosslessBoundaryFixtures`:
- `mkv`: the B-frame recipe remuxed with `-c copy` to `.mkv`, whose video starts after its audio;
- `mov`: the same to `.mov`.

Run the existing `FullSplit_EveryShape…` theory and a final-subset-part case on each. Synthetic fixtures only.

## Acceptance criteria

- [ ] The `mkv` and `mov` shapes pass the full-split per-part checks (first-frame md5 and exact counts) and the final-part subset check.
- [ ] The probe theory asserts that the mkv video `StartTime` is about 0.021-0.023 s with format start 0.
- [ ] No wall-clock assertion. SPEC-001 I66's measured list gains the two shapes.
