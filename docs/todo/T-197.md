## Concept

Document what G-059 changed, as built:

- keyframes from the container's sample table (T-194);
- the probe cache (T-193);
- Exact cut's correctness fixes (T-189, T-190) and its speed (T-191, T-192);
- the lossless boundary fixes: split parts on B-frame sources and cuts on TS/MTS sources land on their keyframe
  (T-219);
- cheaper and exact frame pictures (T-195, T-196);
- the in-repo bench (T-188).

Also say plainly what did **not** get faster and why: the split and join copy passes already run at disk speed
(T-219 changed only where they cut, not their speed).

## Design

- The specs, ADR 0025 with its `docs/adr/README.md` row, and the amendments to ADR 0009 and ADR 0018 were written
  in each behaviour ticket's own commit (legacy docs layout). This task only reconciles the user-facing and reference docs with them.
- Every number quoted comes from a G-059 ticket's Build log, with the fixture named. Nothing is re-measured here.

## Detail

- **`docs/USER_GUIDE.md`:**
  - a 4K MP4/MOV is ready to cut at once, while MKV/TS still indexes in the background;
  - Exact cut decodes from the keyframe before the cut and re-encodes only from the cut to the next keyframe, so a
    late cut is no slower than an early one, and its row bar now moves;
  - an HEVC source, and a file whose timestamps do not start at zero (`.ts`, `.m2ts`, `.mts`), falls back to a
    keyframe-snapped cut; **the row says so before Run** (snap note, the snapped cut, the reason) and after it, or
    in the run summary's *"Not cut exactly"* list when Auto-clear removes the row. For `.ts`/`.m2ts`/`.mts` this is
    described as a correct keyframe-snapped cut only because T-219 shipped;
  - **if you made Exact cuts with an earlier version:** an Exact cut of an **HEVC** source is damaged after the cut
    point, so re-cut it from the original. Under *Replace originals* that original was sent to the Recycle Bin, so
    restore it from there if it is still there. An **H.264** Exact cut from an earlier version has about 80 ms of
    overlapping audio at the cut point; re-cut it if that matters. **Exact cuts of `.ts`/`.m2ts`/`.mts` sources from earlier
    versions repeat about the file's `start_time` (around 1.4 s) at the cut point; re-cut them from the original**
    (wording follows T-190's TS repro Build log);
  - **if you split or cut losslessly with an earlier version** (T-219, wording from its Build log): split parts of a
    source with B-frames ended one GOP late, so each boundary sits one keyframe interval after the one the plan
    showed (the first part is longer, the last shorter, nothing is lost across the set); lossless cuts of
    `.ts`/`.m2ts`/`.mts` sources (a Split subset part, a Bulk Cut row) started one GOP late, so up to one GOP at the
    start of the kept part is missing. Re-split or re-cut from the original if the boundary matters;
  - the split and join copy run at disk speed, so on a slow or shared disk the copy itself takes bytes ÷
    throughput (G-020's default folder is unchanged);
  - cut-point and profile pictures show the cut frame.
- **`docs/ARCHITECTURE.md`:**
  - the keyframe chain: sample table with its two-window anchor, then packet scan, then frame scan;
  - the probe cache and its key;
  - Exact cut as head encode (input seek, x264 veryfast with a quality floor) plus one concat that reads the
    source from the boundary keyframe; the one gate (`TryResolveEncoders`) that decides both the run's fallback and
    the row's display before Run, including the zero-`start_time` rule;
  - the lossless boundary: `-ss` given as source pts minus `start_time`, and the segment muxer's half-frame
    `-segment_time_delta` (T-219);
  - exact versus bucketed frame grabs;
  - the bench.
- **`docs/GLOSSARY.md`:** *Keyframe index (sample table)*, *Exact cut* (two runs; HEVC and sources whose
  timestamps do not start at zero fall back), *Exact-time picture*.
- **`CHANGELOG.md` `[Unreleased]`:**
  - **Fixed:** an HEVC Exact cut wrote video that was corrupt after the joint; an H.264 Exact cut's kept part started
    about 80 ms late with overlapping audio; an Exact cut of a `.ts`/`.m2ts`/`.mts` source repeated about its `start_time`
    (around 1.4 s) at the cut point; cut-point pictures showed a frame up to 1 s before the cut. The entry says what
    to do with files made by earlier versions: re-cut HEVC Exact outputs from the original (under *Replace
    originals*, restore it from the Recycle Bin if it is still there); H.264 Exact outputs have about 80 ms of
    overlapping audio at the cut point; re-cut `.ts`/`.m2ts`/`.mts` Exact outputs from the original.
  - **Fixed, its own entry (T-219):** split parts on B-frame sources ended one GOP late; lossless cuts of TS sources
    (`.ts`, `.m2ts`, `.mts`) started one GOP late. It says what to do with outputs from earlier versions: they are
    valid files with a shifted boundary, so re-split or re-cut from the original if the boundary matters.
  - **Changed:** **Exact cut on an HEVC source now writes the keyframe-snapped lossless cut, and the row says so
    before and after the run; frame-exact cutting for HEVC waits on a later fix (T-201).** The same holds for a
    source whose timestamps do not start at zero (`.ts`, `.m2ts`, `.mts`). MP4/MOV keyframes come from the file's index;
    Exact cut no longer decodes from the file's start and encodes faster, and its row bar and the batch bar move
    during the work; repeated probes are cached; MP4/MOV pictures and the waveform are quicker.
  - Each entry carries its Build-log number, and the entries say that the gains depend on the container (MP4/MOV
    against MKV/TS) and on Exact being chosen.
