## Why / vision
`StagedStatusWiringTests.RunSplit_StatusTextTransitionsThroughStages` fails intermittently — measured **4 fail / 1 pass across 5 isolated runs** on 2026-07-18. It is **pre-existing**, not introduced by the G-019/G-020 drain: the test was last modified at **T-044** (`50cbfc1`, G-013 staged progress), and the drain commits (T-058/T-059/T-061) never touched `OperationViewModel.cs` or the test. It slips through the full suite because a single pass hides it, but it's a real nondeterministic failure.

**Do NOT dismiss it as "just a flaky test."** Per the project's own hard-won lesson (a Flutter "flaky runner hang" that turned out to be a real source bug — unbounded solver loop on bad input), a nondeterministic test failure can be the symptom of a genuine race in the code under test — here, the **status-reporting pipeline** (`OperationViewModel` consuming `IProgress<OperationStatus>` off the ffmpeg run). The investigation must decide which it is before "fixing" the test.

## Gap from current state
The test asserts `StatusText` transitions through the staged sequence (Preparing → Splitting → Finalizing → Done) driven by a fake staged engine. The race is between the `runningStatus` seed and the fake engine's status reports (worker's read) — but that could equally be a real ordering hazard in how `OperationViewModel` marshals progress callbacks to the UI-bound `StatusText`. Unverified which.

## Decisions
- **D1 · Diagnose before fixing** — reproduce reliably (run 20–50×), capture the failing vs passing interleaving, and classify: (a) test-only sync race (the fake/`IProgress` isn't awaited/flushed deterministically in the test) vs (b) product race (`OperationViewModel` can drop/reorder a stage under real timing). 
- **D2 · Fix at the right layer** — if (a), make the test deterministic (synchronous `IProgress`, await the pipeline, or a controllable scheduler). If (b), fix the product marshalling and keep a regression test. Never paper over (b) by loosening the assertion.
