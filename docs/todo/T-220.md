## Concept

Found by the G-059 plan review (round 4, the confirm round) while it checked the frame-count proofs in T-219 and
T-190. It is a third shape of a shifted lossless boundary, after T-219's two, so T-190's stop rule gives it its own
fix ticket instead of a patch inside the Exact work or inside T-219.

- **A per-segment part that has an end runs past it.** `SplitArgsBuilder.PerSegment` emits `-ss <start>` before
  `-i` and `-to <end − start>` (`SplitArgsBuilder.cs:69-100`). Under `-c copy`, `-to` is applied to decoding
  timestamps, so on a B-frame source the part keeps every packet whose dts is before the end. Measured with the
  bundled ffmpeg n7.1.5 on the `MediaFixtures` recipe (libx264 default B-frames, `has_b_frames` 2, `-g 30`):
  - [3, 6) gives **92** frames, against **90** for the same recipe with `-bf 0`;
  - its first 90 frames are md5-identical to source frames 90-179, so the start is right;
  - the two extra frames are source frame 180 (the keyframe at 6 s) and source frame 184 (the P frame coded right
    after it), not frames 180-181;
  - the same part run to the end of the file gives 210 frames, exact.
- **The concat demuxer's `outpoint` does the same.** `outpoint` is a decoding timestamp (ffmpeg docs). A kept middle
  written as [head `duration 0.7`, source `inpoint 4`, `outpoint 9.5`] on a 30 fps B-frame mp4 gives 188 frames
  against 186, and 6.2667 s of video against 6.2 s, with a clean decode. That is the list T-190's two-run Exact
  writes.
- **Who is hit**, on a source with B-frames (the usual x264 and OBS output):
  - every Split subset part that does not run to the end of the file (`SplitEngine.cs:358-361`);
  - every Bulk Cut lossless row with an outro (it reaches `PerSegment` through `KeptMiddleRequestBuilder` and
    `SplitEngine`);
  - every H.264 Exact row with an outro, through T-190's concat `outpoint`.

  The file is valid and plays. It ends about two frames (67 ms at 30 fps) after the planned end, and when the next
  part of the same split is also selected, the two parts share those frames.
- **Why the tests miss it.** `ExactCut_WithBothEnds_KeepsExactlyTheRequestedMiddle` checks the duration within
  0.5 s (`SmartCutEngineIntegrationTests.cs:246-247`), and no test counts a part's frames past its end. T-219 and
  T-190 scope their frame-count checks around this overrun and name this ticket.

## Why later

- **No fix recipe is measured.** Each candidate changes the sacred `-c copy` path and needs frame-count and
  first/last-frame md5 proof across the fixture matrix (B-frame, `-bf 0`, TS, 10 s GOP):
  - write a subset part with the segment muxer, which splits on presentation time (with T-219's half-frame delta),
    and keep only the wanted segment;
  - give `-to` (and the concat `outpoint`) as the end minus the source's reorder delay; this is unproven, and it may
    drop real frames before the end on another GOP structure;
  - a bitstream-level trim of the trailing packets.
- The overrun predates G-059, every part still starts on its keyframe (T-219), and the extra frames lie past a cut
  that is already a keyframe, so nothing the user planned is missing.

## Trigger

- A user reports a part or a cut that runs a few frames past its end, or two selected split parts that share
  frames.
- A later ticket needs a part's last frame to be exact (for example, gapless re-joining of selected parts).
